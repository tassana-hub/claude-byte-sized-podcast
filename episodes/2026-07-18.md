It's live now in Kimi's apps and A.P.I., with full weights due out by the twenty seventh of July. Moonshot says K3 still trails Anthropic's and OpenAI's best models overall, but on its own published evaluations it wins some individual coding and web browsing tests outright, scoring ninety one point two on the BrowseComp benchmark against roughly eighty eight and ninety for Anthropic and OpenAI's flagship models respectively. Those are self-reported numbers that will need independent checking, but speaking in Shanghai this week, China's president declared the country the global leader in open source A.I., adding that development in the field should not be, in his words, a solo performance. Now, over to policy and geopolitics, where the International Energy Agency has published a stark new outlook on critical minerals. China has tripled the number of mineral tariff codes under export control since twenty twenty three, and the agency estimates that six and a half trillion dollars of global economic output, spanning autos, defence, tech and energy, is now at risk if Beijing fully enforces those controls. Meanwhile the renewed war between the United States and Iran has pushed the price of sulphur, a feedstock needed to process copper, nickel and lithium, sharply higher, since roughly half the world's seaborne sulphur trade normally passes through the Strait of Hormuz. Western governments have roughly quadrupled their financing commitments for mineral projects since twenty twenty three to fill the gap left by spooked private investors, and it appears to be working, though the report's own numbers suggest full supply chain independence from China is still decades and several trillion dollars away. Via International Intrigue.

Now, over to community, events and marketing, where the idea worth stealing comes from the hospitality world. Hilton's chief executive, Chris Nassetta, says he dislikes how the industry typically handles guest complaints: someone grumbles at checkout and walks away with, in his words, a bazillion loyalty points. His fix is A.I. that flags problems while a guest is still in the building, so staff can resolve them in the moment rather than paying them off afterwards. It's a useful reframe for anyone running a service business: the goal isn't a better apology system, it's catching the problem before it becomes one. Via Skift.

And the online publication Every offered a candid look at how small teams can now ship real product features without waiting for a roadmap slot. A senior editor there wanted paywall bypassing gift links for the site, got a lukewarm "interesting" from the head of growth, and simply built the feature himself over a couple of afternoons using Codex and Claude, running his own research, writing his own implementation plan, and only pulling in an engineer to review the final pull request. The lesson wasn't really about the feature; it was about what becomes possible once a team decides that small, well scoped ideas no longer need to compete for a stretched engineering team's time. Via Every.

Finally, one interesting thing to take you into the weekend. Economists at M.I.T. have built a new price index for twentieth century America using five point one million product listings from old Sears catalogues, feeding the descriptions through language models to track quality changes that standard inflation measures miss. Their conclusion: real growth in consumer goods between nineteen hundred and nineteen ninety was roughly four times faster than the official numbers suggest, with the gap largest before the Second World War, upending the textbook view that growth was slower in the earlier half of the century than in the postwar boom that followed it. Via Marginal Revolution.
