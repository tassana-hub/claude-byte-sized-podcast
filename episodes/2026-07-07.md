Good morning. It's Tuesday, the seventh of July, and here are seven stories to catch you up.

Chinese AI models are closing the gap with the West. A new analysis argues that last year's DeepSeek moment was not a one off event but the start of a pattern. The newest model from the Chinese lab Zed dot AI, called G L M five point two, now performs close to the leading American closed models on coding and agent tasks, echoing the shock that DeepSeek's earlier release caused within the American AI community. Analysts say this steady catching up has real consequences for the cost of building frontier AI, for the spread of open weight models that anyone can download and modify, and for how Washington sets AI policy going forward. Via CSIS.

Meta says its next model is already rivalling the best from OpenAI. Meta's superintelligence chief, Alexandr Wang, reportedly told staff that a model in training, nicknamed Watermelon, has matched the performance of G P T five point five, while using around ten times the computing power of its predecessor, which only launched in April. Chief executive Mark Zuckerberg separately said progress on AI agents across the whole industry has been slower than expected, though Wang pushed back, saying that was a comment on the industry generally rather than Meta specifically, and promised an update to Meta's existing model with strong coding and agent improvements soon, alongside a coding model to rival Anthropic's best. Via The Rundown.

Washington is moving fast on AI and quantum policy. President Trump has signed an executive order titled Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security, which asks AI companies to voluntarily submit powerful new models for government benchmarking of their cyber capabilities before wider release, with early access going to selected trusted partners chosen with government input. Two further executive orders aim to accelerate American quantum computing research and push federal agencies toward encryption that can resist future quantum based attacks by the end of the decade. Separately, the Federal Communications Commission has expanded a ban on importing telecoms and surveillance equipment from several Chinese manufacturers, citing national security risks, with the wider restrictions due to take effect early this month. Via CSIS.

Twenty twenty six is shaping up as the year of the AI lab. A wave of star researchers are leaving the likes of Meta and Google DeepMind to start their own ventures, and investors are pouring in billions to back them. In Paris, three former Meta and OpenAI staff are seeking four hundred million dollars for a startup called Aire, aiming to build an AI system that can automate scientific research from end to end. Yann LeCun's new venture, AMI Labs, has already raised over a billion dollars in seed funding, while two London based labs, Recursive Superintelligence and Ineffable Intelligence, raised five hundred million and one point one billion dollars respectively in April alone. Via Sifted.

Europe's fintech giants are still in no hurry to go public. Banking software firm Thought Machine says twenty twenty eight now looks like the likely year for its stock market debut, echoing a similar delay announced by Revolut earlier this year, with its chief executive citing a lack of investor appetite for risk after a run of disappointing European listings. Meanwhile, digital bank Starling is cutting around a hundred and thirty jobs as it leans further into automation, and voice AI company ElevenLabs is reportedly in talks for a fresh share sale that would value the business at twenty two billion dollars. Via Sifted.

New figures underline both the toll of the war in Ukraine and the fragility of global trade routes. Combined Russian and Ukrainian casualties have now passed two million, with Russian losses running at close to eight times Ukrainian losses so far this year, a sharp rise from a ratio closer to two or three to one earlier in the conflict. Elsewhere, the Malacca Strait now carries a larger share of the world's seaborne oil trade than the Strait of Hormuz, and more than thirteen million American jobs depend on trade with Canada and Mexico, a reminder of how much economic weight rides on those relationships as trade tensions continue. Via CSIS.

Finally, cyber and undersea tensions are rising too. Israel's cyber chief says Iranian cyberattacks have roughly tripled over the past year, climbing from around sixteen hundred incidents to nearly five thousand, following the recent American and Israeli military operations against Iran. And in the North Atlantic, Britain's defence secretary John Healey has revealed that UK forces tracked Russian attack and spy submarines detected close to the undersea cables that carry much of the world's data and communications, underlining growing concern about threats to that infrastructure. Via CSIS.
