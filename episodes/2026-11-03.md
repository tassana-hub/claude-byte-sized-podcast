Good morning, and welcome to Byte-sized. It is Tuesday the third of November, and today there are six sections, beginning, as ever, in Japan and the Asia Pacific.

Section one, Japan and Asia Pacific.

First, Fukuoka. The city votes for a new mayor on the fifteenth of November, ending sixteen years under Soichiro Takashima. At the latest Kyushu Roundtable, run by Fukuoka Now, forty-six people from seventeen nationalities argued over what growth should actually deliver: better jobs, more affordable housing, better transport, or public spaces that work for everyone. There was enthusiasm for the city's momentum, but real disagreement about who benefits. If you work with Fukuoka's startup community, the next mayor's priorities on housing and transport will shape the city's pitch to foreign talent. Via Fukuoka Now.

Second, Toyota and Waymo. The two companies will test autonomous Toyota bZ4X vehicles in Japan by twenty twenty-seven, then expand to the United Kingdom and other markets. It is a useful signal that Japan is becoming a proving ground for self-driving, backed by its largest carmaker rather than a start-up. Via T L D R Hardware.

Third, Samsung. On its third-quarter earnings call, the company confirmed new two-nanometre chip orders from major cloud providers and A I firms, as its first gate-all-around chips enter production. Its foundry arm is still loss-making, but this is a meaningful step in its fight with T S M C, and a reminder of how much of the A I supply chain runs through Northeast Asia. Via T L D R Hardware.

Fourth, China's robot boom. Chinese embodied A I start-ups raised ninety-three and a half billion yuan across three hundred and twenty-two deals in the first half of this year, about five times the same period last year, with money shifting from robot bodies to the software brains that let robots learn new tasks. Via T L D R Hardware, citing the Japan Times.

Now, over to A I and tech.

Section two, A I and tech.

First, Anthropic's filing. Sifted reports that the company's I P O paperwork shows revenue up by roughly thirteen hundred per cent in a year, hundreds of billions of dollars committed to computing capacity, and a listing expected in the coming months at a valuation of up to two trillion dollars, which would be the largest flotation ever. One reader of the filing notes it recorded its first profitable quarter, and that the real risk is years of locked-in compute bills paid for by customers who can cut back at any time. A European banker told Sifted that anything that is not Anthropic is really hard to list right now. Via Sifted, and T L D R.

Second, data centres have become a political issue. The Rundown reports that roughly twenty American communities are voting on bans or pauses on data centres in this week's midterms, while OpenAI is reportedly preparing to rebrand them as A I compute campuses, and a Democratic House could make life harder for A I firms trying to scale. For anyone selling into infrastructure, local consent is now part of the business model. Via The Rundown A I.

Third, Google's Project Suncatcher has returned the first A I response from orbit, with the open Gemma model writing a haiku from a satellite passing over Svalbard. Power is a real limit for now, with only one of its four chips running, and only in bursts. Two more capable satellites follow in twenty twenty-seven. Via The Rundown A I.

Fourth, OpenAI has released GPT-Rosalind-6, a reasoning model for biology and drug discovery, available to qualified researchers, institutes and government partners. It uses the same weights as its flagship model, rated as high capability in biology and chemistry, and is built to refuse bioweapon requests. Via T L D R A I.

Now, over to startups and venture.

Section three, startups and venture.

First, chips. British start-up Fractile has raised five hundred and forty million pounds in a Series C, according to company filings seen by Sifted, which is a very large round for a European chip company. Via Sifted.

Second, talent. Sifted surveyed companies from its A I one hundred list, and seventy-eight per cent put access to talent among their three biggest growth blockers, ahead of go-to-market and customer adoption. For founders, hiring is the bottleneck, not compute. Via Sifted.

Third, Creandum and Union Square Ventures have led a forty million dollar seed round into a United Kingdom robotics lab that has no product yet. Investors are now backing research teams, not roadmaps. Alongside it, K2 Space has raised five hundred million dollars at about a seven billion dollar valuation, aiming to build around a hundred high-power satellites a year. Via Sifted, and T L D R Hardware.

Now, over to policy and geopolitics.

Section four, policy and geopolitics.

First, the largest crypto heist in history. The F B I blames North Korea for a six point three billion dollar theft from Binance, which has pledged to cover client losses. International Intrigue notes that some stablecoin issuers froze funds immediately, which raises awkward questions about how decentralised crypto really is. Via International Intrigue.

Second, trade. A C S I S analysis finds China's total exports rose five point five per cent last year even as exports to the United States fell about twenty per cent, with displaced goods moving to the European Union. Its argument is that cracking down on transshipment will not fix the American trade imbalance. Via C S I S.

Third, Poland. A constitutional court crisis erupted on Friday when seven justices suspended the court's chief justice, accusing him of helping the president block judicial appointments. President Nawrocki turned his plane around mid-flight and called it a coup, while Prime Minister Tusk says he is restoring the rule of law. Via International Intrigue.

Now, over to community, events and marketing.

Section five, community, events and marketing.

One idea worth stealing, from Indie Hackers: add a powered-by link. Upvoty, a feedback tool, placed a small mention inside customers' feedback portals, where their users would see it. Some of those users wanted the same thing for their own products, and signed up. Any community tool or event page can do the same.

And one event format from Fukuoka Now. Its roundtable pairs two guest commentators with a mixed international audience, then ends with a catered year-end party, so the discussion feeds straight into the socialising. Via Indie Hackers, and Fukuoka Now.

Now, one interesting thing to finish.

Section six, one interesting thing.

From Every, a rerun of Mike Taylor's essay on what A I is teaching us about management. His argument is that the skills that make A I agents reliable, clear briefs, clear goals and checking the work, are the same skills that make human teams effective. So if you want to get better at working with agents, practise managing people well.
