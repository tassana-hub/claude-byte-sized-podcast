Section one, Japan and Asia Pacific.

First, a study with a very Japanese question at its heart: would older people actually choose robot care? Researchers led by Bertrand Achou surveyed more than four thousand Japanese people aged fifty five to seventy five and asked them to pick between two otherwise identical nursing homes, one with entirely human care and one where robots and digital tools handled a big share of one task. Sixty five percent preferred the robot version, and on average they would pay about eight percent more for it. Acceptance was highest for help with mobility, middling for monitoring, and lowest for conversation and companionship. The authors' rough sums suggest the benefits could far outweigh the costs, especially for mobility robots. For anyone building or selling into Japan's ageing economy, the lesson is to start with lifting and moving, not chatting. Via Marginal Revolution.

Second, the Philippines. CSIS's Southeast Asia Program says impeached Vice President Sara Duterte is in real political peril, because the Senate and the Supreme Court have between them lowered the threshold needed to convict and remove her from office. A conviction would reshape the politics of a close Japanese security partner ahead of the next presidential race, so it is one to keep an eye on. Via the CSIS Southeast Asia Program.

Third, a note from the same CSIS briefing that matters in Tokyo as much as anywhere: they put the Chinese defence industrial base on a wartime footing, buying munitions five to six times faster than America. That is the backdrop to every Japanese defence spending debate. Via CSIS.

Section two, AI and tech.

The week's biggest product conversation was OpenAI's developer conference. Every's Dan Shipper sorted its twenty two releases by how much each one helps ChatGPT become your operating system for work. The five that matter most: Dots, an always on agent, and Space, documents that the agent can edit, which together form the workspace; plugin extensions and a subscription usable inside sixteen partner apps, which let other software connect in; and a Decisions interface that he thinks could be the biggest release of all, if it is cheap. His verdict after a week is balanced. The ambition is big, the execution is not there yet, and his own Dot drops messages and loses its connection to his computer. Every's advice is to try it if you already live in ChatGPT, and to stay put for a week or two if you use a rival agent. Via Every.

On the Anthropic side, Every's team says Sonnet five point five has finally found its job. It is the model you steer, for brainstorming, prototyping, design and outlining, at half the price of Opus five point five, while Opus takes the long unsupervised runs. A useful way of thinking about model choice: match the model to how closely you plan to supervise. Via Every.

Two infrastructure notes from TLDR's IT edition. Micron says the memory shortage pushing up server, storage and laptop prices will get worse through twenty twenty seven and twenty twenty eight, with more than seventy five percent of its twenty twenty seven output already committed. If you are planning hardware refreshes, lock in pricing early. And Google's threat intelligence group found that monthly vulnerability disclosures have doubled since early twenty twenty five and exploitation in the wild has nearly doubled, driven by AI tools that turn newly published flaws into working attacks very quickly. Patch windows are shrinking. Via TLDR.

One more from the same issue: Microsoft and Google have joined Salesforce, Snowflake, Oracle, Nvidia and Databricks behind an open source effort to make business definitions portable across analytics and AI platforms. If it works, a metric defined once could be reused in Power BI and BigQuery alike. Via TLDR.

Section three, startups and venture.

Sifted's post summit reporting shows Europe's argument shifting from whether to build late stage funds to how many. The new one-billion-pound UK Scale-Up Fund is still choosing a manager, with a number of venture firms bidding, and some are pushing for American style rules to cut the cost of setting up British funds. At the Sifted Summit, the head of the British Business Bank said the ecosystem should have ten to fifty funds, not one dominant player. And Eurazeo's Hala Fadel put the problem neatly: it is a mega round problem. In rounds of two hundred million to a billion dollars, American investors take board seats and nudge companies to relocate, so the aim is less about Europe only money and more about keeping agency. Via Sifted.

On fresh money, Headline raised four hundred million dollars for its eighth early stage fund, focused on AI startups backing European founders, and London's Dig Ventures raised one hundred and twenty million for AI infrastructure. Via Sifted.

And a culture story: podcaster and investor Harry Stebbings spoke about his regrets over promoting the nine nine six working culture, the seventy two hour week model from China, at the Sifted Summit. It is a sign that the hustle narrative is meeting resistance, even inside venture. Via Sifted.

Section four, policy and geopolitics.

CSIS's By the Numbers gives us four figures worth carrying into any conversation about sanctions and export controls. Russian petrol production is down about twenty percent against last year and diesel nearly thirty percent, so Moscow is restricting fuel exports to protect home supply; yet fossil fuel exports still bring in roughly two hundred and twenty billion dollars a year. Meanwhile American export control fines have already passed two hundred and ninety seven million dollars this year, more than three times the usual five year total, a sign that enforcement, not just rule writing, is now the pressure point for companies with global supply chains. Finally, the Pentagon accounts for about sixty four percent of federal AI contracts and eighty three percent of the money obligated between twenty nineteen and twenty twenty five, so defence is effectively the anchor customer for government AI. Via CSIS.

Section five, community, events and marketing. One idea worth stealing.

Indie Hackers tells the story of Erik Allebest, who bought Chess dot com in two thousand and five for fifty six thousand dollars. It now has close to three hundred million members and is heading towards two hundred million dollars in annual revenue. The tactic is patience: he put community ahead of monetisation for years, served people who already cared, and was ready when the pandemic and The Queen's Gambit pushed chess mainstream. A low single digit paid conversion rate can carry a huge free product. Same newsletter, a small tip: give your longest standing free users a free month of the paid tier. Going, the flight deals newsletter, tried it on people who had been free for over two months and earned more than fifty thousand dollars from one campaign. Via Indie Hackers.

And finally, one interesting thing.

Every's Arielle Shipper has a neat habit for getting better at AI: let your agent coach you. She gave her coding agent a guide to eight levels of AI adoption and asked it to grade her from her own past sessions. It placed her at five point five, told her what would move her up, and now a weekly scheduled task sends her a graded report every Friday. She says it has moved her up more than two levels since June. The idea is that the same tool you are learning is also the best teacher of how you use it. Via Every.
