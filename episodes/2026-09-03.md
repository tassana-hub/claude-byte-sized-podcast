JAPAN AND ASIA-PACIFIC

Japan is moving ahead with submarine launched hypersonic missiles, a step that boosts deterrence against China while nudging Tokyo closer to what analysts call nuclear latency at sea. The capability doesn't put a nuclear warhead on the missile, but it signals Japan edging toward the kind of strategic ambiguity that has long defined nuclear armed states, without actually crossing that line. Via CSIS.

Japan and South Korea are also rewriting the logic of nuclear energy security across North East Asia, and doing so increasingly in step with Washington rather than apart from it. Two historic rivals are finding common cause in energy security as the region's power politics shift under the weight of China's rise. Via CSIS.

Over in Seoul, President Lee has unveiled a record budget of nearly six hundred billion dollars for twenty twenty seven, an increase of around thirteen percent, funded largely by a near doubling of corporate tax receipts on the back of the AI boom. He's spending some of that windfall on more AI investment and on support for the workers and regions most exposed to AI disruption, but he's also quietly setting aside around seventy six billion dollars in reserve, in case the boom turns to bust. It's a notably cautious use of a windfall by world standards, even if some opposition figures grumble that he should be paying down debt instead, debt that is, in fact, relatively low by international standards and still falling. Via International Intrigue.

AI AND TECH

Anthropic has released Claude Fable five point one and Mythos five point one, successors that the company says fix many of the complaints levelled at the original Fable. The new model tops the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index with a record score, more than doubling Fable five's performance on scientific research tasks, and Anthropic says typical work now costs around twenty five percent less, though a demanding task run at maximum effort can actually cost around twenty percent more, since the new model tends to write substantially more text along the way. Safety refusals have also dropped sharply, down sixty percent on cybersecurity questions and eighty five percent on basic medical and biology queries. Mythos, a version of the same model with fewer guardrails, is being kept behind tighter access controls, limited for now to screened cybersecurity and life sciences researchers in the United States. Not to be outdone, OpenAI's upcoming Astra model is said to be the company's first to cross what it calls a critical cybersecurity capability threshold, able to find and exploit unknown security flaws without step by step human guidance, though OpenAI says it will limit access to those capabilities when the model launches. Via TLDR and The Rundown.

Apple has a new chief executive. John Ternus, the company's former hardware chief, has officially taken over from Tim Cook, and used his first staff memo to hype a huge launch event on September the ninth, widely expected to unveil Apple's first foldable iPhone alongside a broader overhaul of Siri aimed squarely at ChatGPT and Claude. Via TLDR.

Brussels has designated ChatGPT as a very large online search engine under the Digital Services Act, while Reddit and Roblox have been classified as very large online platforms. All three now have four months to comply with new obligations around systemic risk assessment, transparency and independent audits. Via TLDR.

And Uber is cutting around three thousand three hundred jobs, roughly ten percent of its global workforce, flattening management layers to redirect investment toward ride sharing, delivery and robotaxis. Via This Week in Startups.

STARTUPS AND VENTURE

Coding startup Cognition is closing in on a fresh funding round of around one billion dollars, at a valuation of roughly forty seven billion dollars, with investor demand reportedly outstripping even that, meaning the final raise could end up larger still. The company is now bringing in more than nine hundred million dollars in annualised revenue. Via TLDR.

Elsewhere, TLDR's founders newsletter describes what investors are calling the new valley, the stall zone between five and twenty five million dollars in annual recurring revenue, where AI companies with impressive growth curves increasingly struggle to convince investors they're truly differentiated rather than just the current leader in a crowded field of copycats. Data from Carta shows just over fifteen percent of twenty twenty two seed companies reached a Series A within two years, roughly half the thirty percent rate for the twenty eighteen cohort. Via TLDR.

And in the Nordics, Sifted's latest leaderboard of the region's hundred fastest growing companies has Stockholm once again dominating, powered by the legal AI firm Legora and the vibe coding platform Lovable, alongside Daniel Ek's body scanning venture Neko Health at the very top. The more surprising trend in the data is energy: nearly one in ten of the hundred companies on the list are energy startups, led by the Norwegian hydrogen firm Hystar and the Danish electricity trader Reel, even as climate tech more broadly falls out of favour with venture capital. The read from founders on the ground is that the surge of renewables onto Nordic grids has created exactly the kind of price volatility that older energy incumbents are struggling to manage, and new entrants are stepping into the gap. Via Sifted.

POLICY AND GEOPOLITICS

The Shanghai Cooperation Organisation held its annual summit in Bishkek this week, and it was a study in competing visions. Xi Jinping pushed for what he called an equal and orderly multipolar world, effectively an end to any US led hierarchy, while Iran's president lobbied for a dedicated SCO bank to insulate sanctioned members like Iran, Russia and Belarus from the Western financial system. India's Narendra Modi threw cold water on both ideas, wary of Chinese dominance, while also delivering an unusually blunt message to Vladimir Putin in front of the cameras, telling him it was time to move from endless war towards an end to war. Via International Intrigue.

And Germany has formally blamed Moscow for last month's discovery of explosive laden drones at Leipzig airport, including one found near a cargo plane loaded with arms bound for Ukraine. Berlin has announced a series of retaliatory diplomatic measures, including the closure of Russia's consulate in Bonn. Via International Intrigue.

Meanwhile the United States and Iran have exchanged fire again in their most intense clash in weeks, with Tehran targeting American sites in Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait and Iraq, and Washington resuming direct strikes on Iranian territory. Both sides are now warning of further escalation, and the price of Brent crude has jumped by more than four dollars a barrel as a result. Via International Intrigue.

COMMUNITY, EVENTS AND MARKETING

One idea worth stealing this week comes from a data provider called Papermark, which tracked over twenty four thousand pitch decks and more than one million three hundred thousand investor views to work out what actually makes investors keep reading. The headline finding: the median view lasts just seventy seven seconds, not the four minutes most founders assume, and nearly a fifth of investors vanish between the cover slide and slide two, which makes that opening slide the single highest leverage page in the whole deck. The data also found that twelve pages is the sweet spot for completion rates, and that decks opening with traction, rather than financials or product screenshots, get read for longer. Via The VC Corner.

On the community side, gathering expert Priya Parker has been hosting a string of free live video conversations for her Substack community this week, pairing herself with guests for loosely structured, candid sessions built around a single theme rather than a fixed agenda. It's a useful format for anyone building a community from scratch: a live, unscripted conversation costs little to host, gives existing members something to invite friends to, and tends to generate far more goodwill than a polished, pre-recorded webinar ever does. Via Priya Parker's newsletter.

And now, for one interesting thing to close on.

ONE INTERESTING THING

Writers at Works in Progress make an unfashionable case for landfill over recycling. Modern, well engineered landfills capture up to ninety five percent of the methane they produce and are, in most of the developed world, far cleaner than people assume, while recycling plastic can cost well over a thousand dollars for every tonne of carbon dioxide saved, compared with under five hundred dollars a tonne for direct air capture. The one clear exception is metal: recycling aluminium saves up to ninety five percent of the energy needed to make it from scratch, which is why, they argue, municipal recycling programmes should focus on metal and largely leave the rest to a well run hole in the ground. Via Works in Progress.
