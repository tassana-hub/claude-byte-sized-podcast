Section one, Japan and Asia Pacific.

First, a data breach with a Japanese flavour. Airlines including Japan Airlines, A N A and Cathay Pacific have disclosed unauthorised access to passenger reservation data, following a security breach at Amadeus, the booking technology provider that sits behind many carriers. The lesson for anyone in government or enterprise is the familiar one: your exposure is often your supplier's exposure, and a single shared vendor can put several national flag carriers in the same headline. Booking Holdings says its assistants at Agoda and Priceline convert at about twice the rate of customers who do not use them. Asia is turning into the proving ground for A I assisted booking, which matters for anyone thinking about inbound tourism to Japan. Via PhocusWire.

Third, energy security in Korea. A new set of figures from C S I S notes that South Korea imports close to ninety-four per cent of its energy, and that around seventy per cent of its crude oil comes through the Strait of Hormuz. With the Iran war still disrupting supply, it is a vivid illustration of how a single hostile actor can sever the flow by force. Japan has a very similar profile, so expect the same arguments about stockpiles, nuclear restarts and diversified sourcing to keep coming up in Tokyo. Via C S I S.

Fourth, a curiosity on governance. Tyler Cowen flags an unusual succession plan at Anthropic. Each of its seven co-founders will keep a confidential, ranked list of up to ten people who could inherit their seat in a special founder vehicle that steers the company's direction. The point is that what is being passed on is not a chief executive role but a seat at the controls. Via Marginal Revolution.

Section five, community, events and marketing. One idea worth stealing.

First, from Indie Hackers: sell to the agencies that resell your product, rather than to individual users. The founder of an email follow-up tool found that one agency started a trial with a hundred mailboxes and added roughly four hundred and fifty more within weeks, all with a single support contact, because agencies reuse one process across many clients. If you run a community or a product, ask who your natural resellers are. Via Indie Hackers.

Second, a conference format from Every. At its first event, Thesis twenty twenty-seven, in a Brooklyn arts centre, it ran the day across three floors: a main stage for big talks, packed classrooms where staff showed real workflows, and informal spaces in between. Now, one interesting thing.

Section six, one interesting thing.

From The Diff: two economists have used buried coin hoards to measure ancient trade. Coins are minted in one place and radiate outwards as they are spent, so where they turn up shows how closely places were connected. The results fit the idea that the early Muslim conquests cut Europe off from Asian luxury goods, which in turn pushed Europe to develop its own domestic production, a very old example of a trade barrier hurting in the short run and building new industry later. Via The Diff.
