Now, over to community and marketing.

One idea worth stealing this week comes from Priya Parker, writing about a Beverly Hills rewatch and what it reveals about group life. Her point, drawn from a decades old sitcom about people awkwardly thrown together, is that most groups only discover their real norms under mild social friction, not through mission statements or values documents written in a boardroom. If you are building a community or a team, the takeaway is to manufacture small moments of low stakes friction deliberately, rather than waiting for real conflict to reveal what your group actually believes. Via Priya Parker.

And a genuinely useful one for anyone fundraising right now. Whatever the real hit rate looks like once you try it yourself, having a repeatable structure for cold outreach beats reinventing your pitch from scratch every time you sit down to write one. Via The V C Corner.

Now, over to one interesting thing.

This one comes from Works in Progress, pushing back on a popular assumption about medieval childhood. The common story goes that parents in medieval Europe could not afford to get emotionally attached to their children, given how many died young, and so raised them with a kind of practical detachment. The piece argues that is largely a modern projection, and that the historical record, letters, wills and court documents, shows medieval parents grieving, doting on and worrying about their children in ways that look strikingly familiar today. It is a good reminder that the assumption people back then must have felt things differently to us is usually doing more work than the actual evidence supports.
