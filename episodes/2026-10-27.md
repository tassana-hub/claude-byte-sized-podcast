Section one, Japan and Asia Pacific.

First, artificial intelligence travel planning arrives in Japan. Meta's AI agent, called Muse, has launched in Japan, making it the first market outside North America to get it. Alongside the launch, Meta has partnered with Klook, the activities booking platform, so the assistant can turn a conversation about a trip into bookable tours and experiences. For anyone in Japanese tourism or travel technology, it is a signal that the big platforms now see Japan as the proving ground for agent-led booking, and that local operators will want to be listed where the agents are looking. Via PhocusWire.

Second, Kim Jong Un's day trip to China. The North Korean leader visited the Chinese border city of Dandong, where he met Wang Huning, the fourth ranking figure in Beijing, and the foreign minister Wang Yi. International Intrigue's reading is that the visit was about showcasing a new land bridge, lining up positions ahead of China's hosting of the A P E C summit next month, and soothing Beijing's nerves about how close Pyongyang has grown to Moscow. Separately, China's Fifth Plenum began on Monday and runs four days, and is widely expected to focus on party discipline and internal governance rather than big economic announcements. Via International Intrigue.

Third, a gas squeeze in South East Asia. Indonesia has begun curbing natural gas exports to Singapore, saying it needs the supply for its own growing industries. Singapore has tried to calm markets, noting that the cuts amount to less than two per cent of its daily gas demand. But the energy minister in Jakarta had just warned that he would review contracts with uncooperative partners, and Singapore recently threatened to impose costs on Indonesian agribusiness over the region's haze, so there is a real chance this is as much about punishing a neighbour as helping local industry. Via International Intrigue.

Section five, community, events and marketing. One idea worth stealing.

Ask your event what future it is assuming. The Event Design Collective points out that every event quietly assumes people will care about certain issues, that certain skills will matter and that familiar formats will keep working. Before your next design conversation, ask what future this event is assuming, and then ask what happens if that future does not arrive. It is a cheap exercise that surfaces assumptions before they harden into decisions. Via Event Design Collective.

And a format worth borrowing from Fukuoka Now. Their Kyushu Roundtable on the thirtieth of October is built around a single question, what should Fukuoka prioritise after sixteen years under Mayor Takashima, with an architect and a long-time resident joining the publisher. It is free, in English, held in a small room with advance reservations only, and seats are limited. One question, three voices, a hard cap on seats: a simple recipe for a community evening people actually show up to. Via Fukuoka Now.
