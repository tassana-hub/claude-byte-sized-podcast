First, agents are learning to use the telephone. Grok Bot, from SpaceXSI, can now sit on hold, book appointments and negotiate bills on United States numbers. The terms make the user the caller of record, and legally responsible for every call. It is a small step, but it hints at a world of bots phoning bots. Via The Rundown A I.

In Tuesday's midterms, voters backed limits on data centres in thirty-eight of the thirty-nine local measures fully counted, and an exit poll found seventy-six per cent would oppose one in their own community. For anyone building A I infrastructure, power and local consent are now as important as chips. Via The Rundown A I.

Third, security for agents. CrowdStrike has launched an A I Kill Switch that lets security teams cut an agent's access to models through connected gateways, with broader controls such as revoking agent permissions still in development. Via T L D R I T.

The signal for founders is fewer bets, and deeper ones. Via T L D R Fintech, and Sifted.

Now, one interesting thing.

Section six, one interesting thing.

Why Is This Interesting looks at a political embarrassment in South Africa. Before the local elections, the ruling party's candidate lists were still uploading at five to five on the deadline day, and nobody pressed the final submit button. A hundred and eighty-one candidates were left in limbo, and the Constitutional Court eventually intervened, forcing six point six million extra ballots to be printed. The writer's point is that we are bad at judging deadlines that matter most to us, and that procrastination is really about managing mood, not discipline. Via Why Is This Interesting.
